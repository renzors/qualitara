Relay · activity check · design exploration

Four ways to answer “is this account’s activity normal?”

Every screen uses the real seed data, run through an approximation of the SPEC engine: the 7 days to Jul 27, 2026 22:20 UTC against the 8 weeks before, at normal sensitivity. Each direction has a list screen and a detail screen for Metro Collision Centers (15 sites, “worth a look”).

A · Queue

IBM Plex · light + dark · split pane

A support inbox. Accounts are grouped by verdict on the left and the selected account opens on the right. The agent works top to bottom and never loses the list.

Triage (Old Town Barbers open)Metro Collision open
Strong at
  • Fastest path from a ticket to an answer
  • Verdict sentence and “Copy summary” sit up top
  • Most familiar pattern for CS teams
Weak at
  • No portfolio-wide pattern view
  • History is a secondary card

B · Scorecard

Geist · light + dark · matrix

A spreadsheet of accounts × signals. Each cell shows the signed distance from usual, coloured by whether the change is good, bad or neutral. The detail screen is a ledger: one row per signal, each with its history, usual range and distance bar.

Portfolio matrixMetro Collision ledger
Strong at
  • Shows everything at once, including near-misses
  • Shows which signal drives each verdict
  • Site × signal matrix handles 15-site accounts well
Weak at
  • Z-scores need explaining to non-analysts
  • Dense on phones; relies on horizontal scroll

C · Brief

Schibsted Grotesk + Literata · light + dark · document

A written report. The list is a weekly digest in plain sentences. The account page reads like an analyst’s note, with a headline verdict, what changed, where and when, and a summary ready to paste into a ticket. Controls are phrased as a sentence.

Weekly digestMetro Collision brief
Strong at
  • Easiest for non-technical agents and customers
  • States caveats (small samples, open leads) in context
  • Ticket text comes built in
Weak at
  • Needs good sentence templates in the API
  • Slower to scan 20+ accounts

D · Monitor

Barlow Condensed + Red Hat Mono · dark only · timeline

A control-room wall where time runs left to right everywhere. The list shows each account’s weekly verdict history, and a replay scrubber sets “as of”. The account page shows a log-scale timeline with a rolling usual band, signal gauges and a site grid.

Verdict history wallMetro Collision monitor
Strong at
  • Makes the replay (as-of) feature central
  • Shows recurring flags, e.g. accounts that trip every few weeks
  • Log scale keeps the Jun 3 spike readable
Weak at
  • Dark-only; heavier visual tone
  • Rolling history is costly: one engine run per week per account

Recommendation

Use A’s split-pane queue as the frame, since it matches how CS picks up tickets. Build the detail pane from B’s signal ledger (history + range + distance per row) with C’s headline sentence and ticket summary on top. Keep D’s scrubber as the as-of control. Hold the verdict-history wall for v2.

Found while building these

  • Small samples over-flag. Old Town Barbers is “unusual” because 4 of its 9 events came in the evening, and Site J because 2 of 4 did. The off-hours and per-site checks need the same n ≥ 10 minimum that outcome rates have.
  • Flags are frequent over time. Across 21 assessable weeks, 10 of 19 accounts come out “unusual” 3 or more times, and Willow Creek 6 times (see D’s wall). That points to a looser default threshold or a 2-week persistence rule.
  • Volume bands round badly. “12 leads vs usual 1–12” reads as normal. Bands need floor/ceil rounding, which the mockups already use.
  • Open leads mask conversion. Metro’s 7% conversion counts 14 open leads that may convert later. Consider excluding leads younger than N days from the conversion rate.
